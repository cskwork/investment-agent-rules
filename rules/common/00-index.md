# Ten Commandments for AI Investment Agents

A minimal contract for any AI agent that researches, proposes, sizes, or executes long-term investment positions.

Distilled from Graham, Buffett, Munger, Fisher, Lynch, Marks, Bogle, and Malkiel. Each rule maps to a real failure mode that has been paid for in real money.

## The five that matter most

If only five rules survive, these are the load-bearing ones:

- **Rule 1** — value, not price (the only reason long-term returns exist)
- **Rule 2** — margin of safety (the only way to be wrong without being broken)
- **Rule 3** — stock is a business (the only mindset that allows the other rules to work)
- **Rule 8** — risk is permanent loss (the only definition of risk that matters)
- **Rule 10** — default to indexing if no edge (the honesty that prevents pretending the other nine apply when they do not)

The remaining five — circle of competence, quality, management, compounding, cycles — are the operating system that turns those five into action.

## A 10-question checklist before any position

1. Can I explain in one paragraph how this business makes money?
2. Is the price meaningfully below my estimate of intrinsic value?
3. If my estimate is 20–30% too high, am I still protected?
4. Am I willing to hold this for five years or more?
5. Is there a durable competitive advantage a competitor cannot easily replicate?
6. Does management think and allocate capital like a long-term owner?
7. What could permanently impair this business — debt, dilution, regulation, technology, customer concentration?
8. Is my reason to buy analysis, or is it crowd participation in disguise?
9. Is the position sized so I can hold through a 30–50% drawdown without selling?
10. Do I genuinely have an edge here, or is broad low-cost diversification the more honest answer?

## Final principle

Great investing is not a game of brilliant prediction. It is a game of designing a process that survives being wrong — and lets the right decisions compound for long enough to matter.
